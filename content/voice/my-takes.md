# My Takes

## Core worldview

I believe ambitious people should build lives around **agency,
deliberate effort, curiosity, and control of attention**.

My philosophy is not about becoming a productivity machine. It is about
becoming more capable while still having a life worth living.

I believe we should think bigger than what currently seems realistic,
but then become disciplined enough to turn that ambition into action.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. I do not believe in multitasking

I think humans often fool themselves into believing that multitasking
saves time.

In most demanding cognitive work, we cannot give our full attention to
several things simultaneously. When we keep switching between important
tasks:

-   quality drops
-   concentration is fragmented
-   work becomes less satisfying
-   supposed time savings can be misleading

My default principle is:

> **One important thing at a time.**

There is an important distinction, however.

Some forms of multitasking can work when one activity requires
substantial cognitive processing while the other is largely habitual,
automatic, or reflexive.

For example, an activity that requires analytical attention can
potentially be paired with a habitual activity that does not demand much
decision-making.

The problem is not simply "doing two physical things at once." The
problem is asking the brain to perform multiple demanding cognitive
operations at the same time.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Dream ridiculously --- then ground the dream in reality

I believe in **positive thinking, extreme self-belief, and ambitious
dreams**.

I am comfortable with the idea of being "delusional" when that means
having the courage to pursue something that currently looks like a
daydream.

But dreaming alone accomplishes nothing.

My philosophy is:

> **Dream without artificially limiting yourself. Then use reality to
> determine the strategy.**

Ambition should be paired with execution.

Building a strategy is often the easy part. The difficult part is:

-   staying consistent
-   doing the work
-   implementing the strategy
-   adapting when reality disagrees with the original plan

The dream gives direction. Reality gives feedback. Consistent execution
determines how far we actually go.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Extreme self-belief protects curiosity

I think many people spend years seeking external validation.

Eventually, they become afraid of looking foolish, failing publicly, or
trying something unconventional. They quietly kill their own curiosity
before discovering what they might have been capable of.

I want younger people to believe in themselves enough to:

-   pursue bold ideas
-   challenge themselves
-   try things they have never tried before
-   take meaningful risks
-   pursue breakthroughs
-   remain curious

I believe that when a problem exists, we should at least ask:

> **What if there is a solution?**

For things that already exist, we can use evidence to determine what has
been achieved and what approaches have worked.

For genuinely novel problems, there may be no previous roadmap. That is
where extreme self-belief and determination become especially important.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Delusion needs evidence when evidence exists

I do not believe that self-belief means ignoring reality.

When something has already been demonstrated, we can use evidence to
determine:

-   whether it is possible
-   what has already been achieved
-   what constraints exist
-   which strategies have worked
-   what needs to change

But when pursuing something genuinely novel, there may be no existing
evidence that tells us exactly how to succeed.

In those situations, someone has to be willing to believe before the
evidence exists.

> **Evidence tells us what has been demonstrated. Imagination allows us
> to attempt what has not yet been demonstrated.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Failure is part of the process

I do not measure a meaningful pursuit only by its final outcome.

If someone dedicates years to a difficult goal and genuinely does
everything within their capabilities to pursue it, those years can still
be extraordinarily valuable even if the final outcome is failure.

Failure teaches us how to become successful faster than success itself
can.

Failure can reveal:

-   what does not work
-   where our assumptions were wrong
-   what skills we lack
-   what constraints we underestimated
-   how we need to adapt

The goal is not to eliminate failure.

The goal is to make failure **informative**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Mediocrity is often self-imposed

I see mediocrity as more than simply achieving average results.

To me, mediocrity can come from:

-   lack of discipline
-   lack of ambition
-   killing one's potential before reaching its peak
-   choosing short-term comfort over long-term growth
-   constantly seeking validation
-   becoming dependent on shortcuts and hacks
-   refusing to leave the comfort zone

The dangerous part is not being average at something today.

The dangerous part is deciding that comfort is more important than
discovering what you are capable of becoming.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Take calculated risks outside the comfort zone

When I talk about taking risks, I do not mean reckless behaviour.

I mean being willing to take on difficult, unfamiliar challenges even
when they are inconvenient.

For example:

-   learning a new language while pursuing a demanding professional
    degree
-   learning an instrument
-   taking leadership responsibility
-   developing a new skill outside one's primary field
-   attempting to compete with people who have been doing something for
    years

These choices require guts because there is a real possibility of
looking inexperienced.

But that is exactly where growth can happen.

I believe we often underestimate what we can develop through deliberate
training and sustained effort.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. I believe strongly in neuroplasticity

I believe the brain is capable of learning and adapting.

I therefore believe that abilities that initially appear far outside our
current capability can sometimes be developed through deliberate
practice, appropriate training, repetition, and sustained effort.

I do not want people to assume that their current level of ability is
their permanent ceiling.

> **Current ability is a starting point, not necessarily a final
> limit.**

Extraordinary results can include academic achievement, physical
capability, intellectual development, creative ability,
entrepreneurship, technical skills, leadership, and learning across
multiple disciplines.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Technology should be an enabler, not an owner

I do not believe technology itself is the enemy.

We live in a connected world, and social media and technology can
provide enormous value.

The problem is losing control over how we use them.

My principle is:

> **Use your phone. Your phone should not use you.**

Technology should expand human capability rather than continuously
consume human attention.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. We cannot beat attention algorithms through willpower alone

Modern digital platforms are extremely effective at capturing attention.

I do not think humans should assume that willpower alone is enough to
compete with increasingly sophisticated attention-optimization systems.

We need **friction and environmental constraints**.

That can include:

-   app blockers
-   notification limits
-   removing unnecessary apps
-   tracking screen time
-   creating physical distance from the phone
-   deliberately designing the digital environment

This is not weakness.

> **Designing your environment is itself a form of discipline.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Focalyst is about ownership of time

Focalyst should represent more than a collection of productivity
features.

Its core idea is:

> **Own Your Time.**

I see Focalyst as:

> **A personal operating system that helps you take control of your
> life.**

The app blocker is particularly important within this philosophy.

If social media and "brain-rot" apps are consuming valuable time,
creating an obstacle between the person and the app can make intentional
behaviour easier.

The goal is not to make people dependent on Focalyst forever.

The goal is to help them regain control over their attention and build
behaviours that eventually become more automatic.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Responsibility for digital distraction is shared

If someone spends six hours a day scrolling, I do not think
responsibility belongs entirely to the individual.

My current way of thinking about it is roughly:

-   **50% environment**
-   **30% platform**
-   **20% individual**

The exact percentages are less important than the principle: behaviour
is shaped by the environment in which it occurs.

The individual still has responsibility.

They should:

-   track screen time
-   understand their patterns
-   create constraints
-   use tools such as app blockers
-   deliberately redesign their environment

But we should not pretend that a human brain is operating in a neutral
environment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Productivity means control, not maximum output

I do not define productivity as doing the maximum possible amount of
work.

I define it as:

> **Having greater control over your time so you can spend it on the
> things that actually matter.**

That includes:

-   meaningful work
-   learning
-   health
-   relationships
-   family
-   friendships
-   experiences
-   exploration
-   rest

A person who completes 14 hours of work but has no control over their
life is not necessarily more productive than someone who completes six
focused hours and uses the remaining time intentionally.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Extreme ambition should not become obsession

I believe extreme ambition can provide motivation without requiring
constant motivational content.

When the goal itself matters deeply, you should not need to watch
another motivational video every morning to convince yourself to work.

But ambition should not become destructive obsession.

Many pursuits have delayed rewards. You can work extremely hard for
months without immediately seeing money, recognition, academic results,
business growth, or visible transformation.

If someone becomes obsessed while constantly expecting immediate
rewards, the gap between effort and reward can create frustration.

The pursuit needs meaning beyond instant gratification.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. I reject the "5 AM or you are failing" philosophy

I do not believe waking up at 5 AM is a universal requirement for
success.

People need sufficient sleep.

My current view is that roughly **6--7 hours of sleep** should be
treated as a practical minimum target, rather than glorifying sleep
deprivation.

I also reject the idea that an ambitious person should lock themselves
away and work on one project at the expense of everything else.

Humans are social animals.

We need:

-   friends
-   family
-   relationships
-   experiences
-   physical activity
-   new environments
-   both good and difficult experiences

You do not know how long you will live.

> **Do not become so focused on building a future that you forget to
> live the present.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Do not wait for the perfect moment

I do not believe meaningful change needs to begin on January 1, Monday,
the next semester, or after life becomes less busy.

If something matters, start.

The ideal person does not wait for a new year to begin something
meaningful.

They act when they recognize the need for change.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Track behaviour until it becomes automatic

I believe tracking is particularly useful when building new behaviours.

A person can deliberately track activities such as:

-   gym sessions
-   swimming
-   study blocks
-   habits
-   screen time
-   focused work

The purpose is not to track everything forever.

The purpose is to make the behaviour visible until repetition makes it
increasingly automatic.

A useful example is brushing your teeth. You normally do not need a
motivational speech or detailed reminder to do it. It has become part of
the automatic structure of daily life.

The objective of habit systems is to move useful behaviours in that
direction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. AI is an amplifier, not a replacement for thinking

I do not believe people should be afraid of AI.

We should adopt it where it improves efficiency and capability.

But there is an important boundary:

> **Use AI to increase your capabilities, not to outsource your ability
> to think.**

Over-reliance on AI can weaken the very abilities we are trying to
develop.

People should continue to:

-   think independently
-   question outputs
-   solve problems
-   write
-   create
-   reason
-   make decisions

AI should be an amplifier of human intelligence, not a substitute for
it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. A meaningful life is bigger than productivity

I do not want productivity to become another form of self-imposed
imprisonment.

The objective is not:

> Work more → optimize more → achieve more → repeat forever.

The objective is to build enough control that you can pursue meaningful
goals while still experiencing life.

A successful life can contain:

-   ambitious projects
-   difficult work
-   learning
-   failure
-   friendships
-   family
-   travel
-   fitness
-   curiosity
-   play
-   relationships
-   unexpected experiences

You should not reach the end of your life having achieved everything on
your task list but regretting everything you did not experience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. The person I want to help create

The ideal person shaped by this philosophy is someone who:

-   controls their desire to doom-scroll
-   plans their day before it begins
-   reflects weekly on whether they are moving in the right direction
-   does not wait for January 1 to change something
-   tracks important behaviours until they become habits
-   focuses on one demanding task at a time
-   thinks ambitiously
-   takes calculated risks
-   is willing to look inexperienced while learning
-   treats failure as information
-   believes current ability is not necessarily the final ceiling
-   uses technology deliberately
-   adopts AI without surrendering independent thinking
-   protects sleep and relationships
-   continues to have experiences outside work
-   pursues extraordinary goals without sacrificing the entirety of
    their life

Ultimately, I want people to become **more capable, more self-directed,
and more alive**.
